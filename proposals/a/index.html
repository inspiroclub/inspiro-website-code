          Why it works

          We don't ask anyone to quit their phone. We give them a better reason to put it down for an evening. Here is what research says, including where it is weaker than headlines suggest.

          20–40minutes less screen time a day

          Making the phone duller works

          Switching a phone to greyscale cut daily use by about 39 minutes in one study of US students and by about 20 minutes in a pre-registered one-week Dutch study.

          Holte and Ferraro, 2020; Dekker and Baumgartner, 2023.

          Fullenergy restored by a nature break

          A real break beats a scrolling break

          In an experiment with 308 employees, a short break in nature fully restored energy. A social media break did so only partly; no break was worst.

          Grobelny and colleagues, Scientific Reports, 2024.

          57%of students would prefer a world without TikTok

          Many people want to step away, together

          US college students said they would rather nobody used TikTok (57%) or Instagram (58%). Leaving alone feels costly; leaving as a group doesn't.

          Bursztyn and colleagues, American Economic Review, 2025.

          Mixedresults for digital detox

          Why we offer alternatives, not abstinence

          Reviews of digital detox studies find mixed effects on mood and well-being, and cravings during abstinence. So we don't run detox challenges as the main offer; we make offline time worth choosing.

          Radtke and colleagues, systematic review, 2022.

          Several of these studies are listed in the further reading for Steven Bartlett's October 2026 episode with Dr. Sahar Yousef. Each will be checked against the original paper before this page goes live.

          A working day that ends on time
